BATTLEFIELDS NOTES
Zoids Mercenaries, November 2026

This pass replaces the three open maps with four battlefields built for
cover fighting in a MechWarrior 5 style: an abandoned city, a dense jungle,
a sparse jungle and a rocky mountain pass. Terrain and cover now stop
shots and line of sight, water changes how Zoids move, cool and shoot, small
props break, and enemy pilots find their way around all of it.

All battlefield rules live in js/battlefield.js, which has no three.js in it,
so the node tests and the game use the same data and maths. js/maps.js only
draws what js/battlefield.js generates. Everything you see is procedural:
no new models, textures or sounds were added, so CREDITS.txt, README.txt and
the hangar credits button are unchanged (the existing CC-BY credits stay as
they were).


1. AUDIT OF THE OLD MAPS

Desert (Red Dune Expanse), plains (Verdant Reach) and arctic (White Shelf)
were one rolling heightfield each with a few rocks, two or three decorative
ridges and a handful of boxes for the assault outpost. Problems found:
  Cover did nothing for line of sight. Missile locks and AI fire went
  straight through rocks and hills; only the shot ray hit meshes.
  Shots raycast against every map mesh, which would be far too slow with
  real cities or forests (about 9,000 triangles tested per ray).
  Ridges had no collision; only round rocks and box buildings did.
  Spawns were fixed points with no check, so a unit could start inside a
  rock if the layout changed.
  AI pilots drove straight at the target and only reversed when stuck.
  No water, no destructibles, one sky colour and one flat fog per map.

Old contract ids still load: desert and arctic open the Rocky Mountain Pass,
plains opens the Sparse Jungle (BIOME_ALIAS in js/battlefield.js).


2. THE FOUR BATTLEFIELDS

All maps: playable box 1224 m square (units clamp at +-612 m), drawn ground
2000 m square with distant backdrop (skyline, hills or snow peaks). Fixed
clear zones keep the mission objectives open: spawn (0, 60) r 82, centre
r 42, the three survey beacons r 26, the assault outpost (0, -80) r 58, and
the convoy start and exit (0, +-300). The convoy road runs along x = 0 from
z = 340 to z = -330 and is 28 m wide.

Abandoned City
  Blocks on a 70 m grid, 44 m square, so streets are 26 m wide; the
  central avenue is 68 m wide between block faces (28 m paved road).
  Per block: 10% empty lot, 18% collapsed (rubble heap plus a fallen slab
  tilted 60-75 degrees), towers 32-58 m tall, or 2-4 mid-rise buildings
  10-28 m tall; 40% of buildings are visibly broken. A skyline ring of
  buildings stands outside the play box.
  Seed 1001: 376 building pieces, 34 rubble heaps, 70 cars and barriers,
  480 cover pieces in all.
  Hazards: 6 clouds alternating smoke and dust, placed over burning wrecks
  (flickering flames). Water: a flooded canal across the map at z = -150,
  22 m wide, 2.6 m deep. The convoy wades through it.
  Look: dusty amber sky, fog density 0.0052, warm low sun.

Dense Jungle
  About 285 big trees (trunks 12-17 m, 1.6-2.7 m radius, canopy blobs on
  top), 180 saplings, 260 undergrowth bushes, 18 boulders. Trees also
  crowd the outer 45% of the clear zones, so the drop zone is a small
  clearing, not a lawn. Trunks, mist and fog keep sightlines short.
  Hazards: 7 mist banks out to 320 m. Water: a winding river around
  z = 195 (26 m wide, 2.4 m deep) and a pool (r 22).
  Look: green-grey sky, fog density 0.0078, soft sun.

Sparse Jungle
  26 tree clusters (3-7 trees each) around open clearings, 40 lone giants,
  70 saplings, 140 bushes, 260 grass tufts, 26 boulders. 243 cover pieces.
  Long sightlines across the clearings.
  Hazards: 3 mist banks. Water: two ponds (r 30 and r 24) and two streams.
  Look: blue sky, light haze (fog density 0.0036), bright sun.

Rocky Mountain Pass
  A winding canyon: centre line x = 26 sin(0.0065 z + 0.8), floor 110-265 m
  wide, with two chokepoints about 110 m wide at z = 200 and z = -270.
  The walls are an 8 m talus slope, then cliffs that climb 3.4 m per metre
  to about 95 m; anything steeper than 3 m above the talus is a wall ground
  Zoids cannot climb (they slide back). Two 5.5-7.5 m rock ridges cross
  half the floor (one each side, gap at the road) for hull-down fighting.
  8 rock spires (24-46 m tall), 64 boulders on the floor (the small ones
  break). Snow only on the far backdrop peaks.
  Hazards: 5 dust clouds. Water: two meltwater pools (r 19) at the cliff
  feet.
  Look: deep blue sky, fog density 0.0034, hard white sun.

Scale checks (tests/battlefields.test.mjs): the whole convoy road and
every clear zone are free for a Death Saurer (radius 5.4 m); more than 55%
of sampled city side-street points fit one; both chokes fit one; the
tallest cover (58 m) is under the Pteras ceiling (70 m above the ground).


3. COVER AND LINE OF SIGHT

Cover is data, not meshes: circles (trees, saplings, boulders, spires) and
boxes (buildings, rubble, cars, barriers), each with a base and top height,
kept in a 32 m spatial grid.
  Shots: the shot ray tests the cover pieces analytically through the grid
  (rayBlockers) and the terrain by marching the heightfield in 3 m steps
  with a 7-step bisection (rayGround). Only Zoid parts still go through
  the three.js raycaster. Shells and beams stop on the first thing hit.
  Line of sight: lineBlocked() samples the terrain every 4 m and tests the
  cover pieces along the line. Used by missile locks (SEEKER BLOCKED:
  COVER or TERRAIN), AI fire and the AI cover search.
  Collision: units are pushed out of cover pieces and slid back off cliffs.
  Pteras flies over cover (its floor is the top of the cover under it).

Destructible props: cars (30 hp), barriers (60 hp), saplings (35 hp) and
small boulders (90 hp, under 2.8 m radius). Any shot or beam damages
them; a Zoid at least as wide as the prop (crush radius 3.0-4.0 m)
walks straight through and flattens it. Gojulas, Death Saurer and the other
big Zoids crush cars and saplings underfoot. Buildings, big trees, big
rocks and spires are indestructible.


4. WATER AND HAZARDS

Water (WATER in js/battlefield.js)
  Speed: x(1 - 0.4 * min(1, depth / max(1.5, 0.9 * radius))). Deep water
  costs a light Zoid 40% of its speed; a Death Saurer in 2 m of water
  loses about 16%. Flyers in the air are not affected.
  Cooling: weapons cool 50% faster while standing in 0.3 m or more of
  water. The HUD shows WADING, COOLING +50%.
  Beams: under the surface a beam loses power fast (absorb 0.09 per m,
  scatter 0.004 rad per m), like a very thick cloud. Shells, missiles and
  seekers ignore water.
  Spawns never land in water deeper than 1.2 m.
Clouds (HAZARD_KINDS in js/weaponsys.js)
  City smoke (absorb 0.03/m) and dust (0.024/m), jungle mist (0.026/m),
  pass dust. Beams bend and lose power through them and 12 m of cloud on
  the line of sight blocks a missile lock, as in the weapons overhaul.


5. SPAWNS

Every spawn request goes through findFreeSpot(): if the requested point is
inside cover, on a cliff or in deep water, it walks a spiral outwards in
4 m rings until it finds a dry, free spot for that Zoid's radius (plus
1.5 m). Scripted units (convoy trucks, outpost turrets, generator) keep
their exact places inside the clear zones. The node test checks every
place the missions actually spawn units (wave lines ahead of the player,
the outpost, 40 escort ambush layouts) on four maps and three seeds: no
invalid spot, under 5% moved more than 40 m, and every spot can walk to the
player start.


6. AI NAVIGATION

Enemy pilots get a nav helper from the mission (ctx.nav):
  LOS: checked from the pilot's eye to the target's aim point, cached for
  0.25 s. Without sight the pilot holds fire; after 1.5 s unseen it pushes
  toward the target to regain contact.
  Routes: when the straight walk to the target (or to a cover spot) is
  blocked, the pilot plans a route with A* on an 8 m grid (8 neighbours,
  no corner cutting, deep water costs 3x), string-pulled to a few
  waypoints. Re-planned every 1.5 s or when the goal moves 25 m. The pilot
  heads for the furthest waypoint (up to 3 ahead) it can walk to straight
  (checked every 0.3 s). The grid is built lazily per size class (normal
  and superheavy), about 30 ms in the city, and dropped when cover is
  added.
  Steering: feelers at 1.5 m, 3.5 m, 45% and 100% of 10 + 2 x radius m
  ahead, trying 0, +-20, +-40, +-60, +-86 and +-120 degrees off the wanted
  heading, the flank side first.
  Cover: when its hottest weapon passes 78% heat the pilot looks for a spot
  behind hard cover (4 m or taller, not destructible) within 70 m that
  hides it from the target, goes there, and comes out when heat drops
  under 30% or after 6 s (cooldown 3 s to look again, 8 s after leaving).
  Stuck: no progress for 0.8 s means reverse and flip the flank side;
  stuck twice within 5 s means a 1.6 s break-away on a fresh heading.
  Flyers, turrets and bracing charge-cannon pilots skip all of this.
In the node test a pilot following the same rules reaches 8 of 8 far
goals on every map for a normal and a superheavy Zoid with no stalls; in
the browser test, enemies 150-180 m away close to 60-90 m in 20 s on every
map with 0 stuck events and never inside cover.


7. LOOK

Per map: sky dome with a vertex gradient (top and horizon colours), a sun
disc, hemisphere light colours, sun colour, direction and strength, fog
colour and density (all in LOOKS in js/battlefield.js). The ground has
vertex colours for low and high ground, cliff faces and mud under water.
City facades use a procedural canvas texture (windows, some lit, some
blown out); the avenue uses a procedural asphalt texture (lane lines,
cracks, patches). Water is a translucent surface. Instanced meshes draw
trees, saplings, bushes, grass, boulders and spires; the city is two
merged meshes plus instanced cars.

Minimap: a static layer drawn once per mission at 0.5 px per m (cliffs,
water, indestructible cover), rotated with the player under the live
contacts.


8. PERFORMANCE

Measured in headless Chrome with SwiftShader (software GL), 1280 x 720,
average of 40 frames in the browser test:
  Final npm test run: Abandoned City 65.8 ms, Dense Jungle 57.7 ms,
  Sparse Jungle 51.7 ms, Rocky Mountain Pass 56.8 ms (budget 100 ms;
  earlier runs 42-67 ms). tests/glb_browser.mjs (escort in the city)
  69.2 ms, tests/shield_anim_browser.mjs 73.5 ms.
How:
  Shots and LOS use analytic cover tests, not mesh raycasts.
  Lambert materials everywhere, merged city meshes, instancing for all
  vegetation and rocks, a coarse skirt beyond the play box with the part
  under the detailed ground removed, and draw order cover, ground, skirt,
  sky so hidden pixels are skipped.
  MSAA is off on software GL and on high-DPI phones (js/quality.js). On
  SwiftShader 4x MSAA alone cost about 35 ms of a 100 ms city frame.
  Real GPUs keep MSAA. ?aa=1 or ?aa=0 in the URL forces it on or off.
  The dense jungle draws one canopy blob per tree (two cost about 8 ms).
Without the MSAA change the new maps measured 95-115 ms in the same
harness, so the budget depends on it on software GL.


9. TESTS

tests/battlefields.test.mjs (node): field generation time, cover and
hazard counts, old biome aliases, wading rules, clear zones and the convoy
road free for superheavies, real spawn requests valid and reachable,
beams weakened through water, building and canyon wall blocking LOS and
shots, a cover spot found behind a building, pass width and cliffs, city
street width, A* plus steering reaching 8 far goals per map and size.
tests/battlefields_browser.mjs (browser, each map forced in turn):
frame time under 100 ms, no unit spawned inside cover, the game's own shot
ray stopped by cover, missile lock blocked by cover, canyon wall stopping a
shot, wading slowing the player (about 25% less distance in 2 s), a prop
destroyed, live AI closing in without getting stuck, and screenshots in
screenshots/battlefields/ (wide, third person, cockpit and wading per map).


10. KNOWN LIMITS

Buildings, big trees and spires cannot be destroyed.
Fallen slabs and leaning ruins collide as upright boxes.
The minimap layer does not update when a prop breaks (props are not on it).
AI routes are planned on an 8 m grid, so pilots keep about 4 m off walls
and will not squeeze through gaps narrower than about 9 m.
The convoy trucks drive straight down the road at their fixed speed,
including through the city canal (2.6 m deep); water does not slow them.
